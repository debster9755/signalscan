01 — What it is

A decision instrument, not an autonomous consultant

SignalScan takes a client's campaign context and evidence, maps how campaigns actually get made, works out what the brand really sounds like, compares it against two competitors, and produces one scored, cited recommendation with a business case and a 30-day pilot charter.

Software structures. Humans decide.

The system organises evidence and does the arithmetic. A named person approves every recommendation before it reaches a client.

Five business days

Intake to approved recommendation and pilot charter — not a six-week consulting engagement.

One answer, not a menu

Five to eight candidates are scored; exactly one is recommended to fund first.

02 — Problems it solves

What it is actually for

“We have twelve AI ideas and no way to choose”

Workshop enthusiasm produces a wish-list ranked by whoever spoke last. Scoring is weighted, explicit and reproducible — the same evidence yields the same rank on any machine.

Pilots that die at month six

No accountable owner, no measurable outcome, or data the client was never allowed to release. Hard stops surface all of it on day four, before the budget is committed.

Business cases nobody can defend

A missing cost becomes a plausible-looking placeholder, and the number collapses under finance review. Here a missing input stays missing and is reported as such.

Automating the wrong half

Teams automate the work when the cycle is mostly queue. Flow mapping separates hands-on effort from waiting before anything gets scored.

03 — How it leverages AI

Narrowly, and never for the number

Reads evidence

Extracts brand rules, workflow detail and competitor positioning from client documents — every claim tied to a page or slide it can point at.

Infers when there is no guideline

With no brand book, rules are inferred from roughly 25 labelled campaigns and held as pending until a human approves them.

Drafts, never decides

Proposes candidate workflows and drafts report narrative. Ranking, banding and arithmetic happen in code.

No model ever produces a score, a priority band, or a business-case figure. The scoring package has no model dependency at all — which is why a recommendation cannot drift between two runs, and why every number can be traced to its inputs.
04 — Top business benefits

Why an executive sponsors this

One funded decision, in a week

A twelve-idea wish-list becomes a single recommendation with an owner, a KPI and a 30-day charter.

Non-viable ideas die cheaply

Hard stops block a candidate outright, whatever it scored — before pilot spend, not after.

A business case finance will sign

Nothing is estimated to make a slide look finished. Gaps are named, not filled.

Survives brand, legal and procurement

Every claim carries a citation to the client's own evidence; a named human approves the recommendation.

Reproducible under challenge

Integer-exact server arithmetic. Same evidence, same answer — on any machine, in any month.

Client evidence isolated by construction

Tenant separation is enforced in the database by row-level security, not by a query filter someone might forget.

07 — Four analysis stages carry the work

Where the effort actually goes

Evidence ingestion

Parses uploads, chunks them, and attaches a citable location — page, slide, sheet — to every excerpt.

Campaign-flow mapping

Normalises three capture paths into one stage schema and finds where the real friction sits.

Brand & competitor analyst

Extracts guideline rules, or infers them from ~25 campaigns, then compares against exactly two competitors.

Opportunity scoring

Scores candidates deterministically on the server across 16 weighted factors — never in a prompt.

08 — Agentic workflow

Inputs it takes, outputs it gives

Inputs
  • 20 intake answers — business, flow, data, brand, risk
  • Evidence files — PDF, DOCX, PPTX, XLSX, CSV, images; ≤50 MB each, ≤200 per scan
  • ~25 campaigns, labelled on-brand / off-brand
  • Brand guidelines, where they exist
  • Two competitors, public evidence only
  • Workflow timings — work, elapsed, wait, rework per stage
Agentic middle
  • Cited extraction — two passes, so a location is verified rather than asserted
  • Schema-validated normalisation, one repair attempt, then needs review
  • Retrieval filtered by tenant before similarity
  • Deterministic scoring, confidence and hard stops in code
Human gates: operator validates the flow · reviewer approves brand rules · sponsor confirms competitors · strategist approves the recommendation
Outputs
  • Validated flow map with friction ranked by wait, workload and rework
  • Approved brand rule set, each rule cited
  • Competitor differentiation across eight axes
  • 5–8 scored opportunities in four priority bands
  • Hard-stop register — what is blocked and what would clear it
  • Business case — hours, gross value, year-one net, payback
  • 30-day pilot charter for the single recommendation

Cost is tokens, not compute

Two-pass extraction over 200 documents is the bill. Prompt caching on the stable prefix and routing classification to a small model are the levers that matter.

Postgres is sufficient

Thousands of chunks per assessment, not millions per query. pgvector holds at this scale; add an index before adding a vendor.

Isolation is per workspace

One business unit cannot read another's evidence — enforced by row-level security, and proven by tests that connect as the real application role.

Intel and Toshiba are named here only as recognisable size archetypes. They are not customers, and none of the figures above come from their systems or their data.
